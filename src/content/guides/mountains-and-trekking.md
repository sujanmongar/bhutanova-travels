Bhutan is one of the most mountainous countries on Earth. Mountains are considered the homes of deities, and climbing peaks above 6,000 m is prohibited — which is why **Gangkhar Puensum (7,570 m)** remains the world's highest unclimbed mountain.

## Notable peaks
| Peak | Height |
|---|---|
| Gangkhar Puensum | 7,570 m |
| Jomolhari | 7,326 m |
| Masang Gang | 7,165 m |
| Jichu Drake | ~6,800 m |

## Popular treks
- **Druk Path** — 4–6 days, moderate, Paro to Thimphu past high lakes
- **Dagala Thousand Lakes** — 5–6 days, moderate
- **Jomolhari** — 7–9 days, challenging, to the base of Jomolhari
- **Laya–Gasa** — 10–12 days, challenging, via the remote Layap villages
- **Snowman Trek** — ~25 days, one of the toughest treks in the world

## Altitude
Take it slowly, drink plenty of water and plan an acclimatisation day in Paro before any high trek. Tell your guide straight away about headaches, nausea or breathlessness.
